# Product Vision Document


### What semantic memory should allow Kivi to become

Dictation turns speech into text. Hey Kivi answers one request and forgets it.
Semantic memory is the difference between an assistant that hears a person once
and one that has been listening all along.

What it should learn is not how the person talks to Kivi. It is how the person
deals with the world — who they work with, what they promised, how they write
to their manager against how they write to their mother. Kivi is a witness to
that, not a party to it. Get this right and "ask Appa if he took his medicine"
works without first explaining who Appa is, or how they speak to him.

### Where the real value is created

In not making a person repeat themselves. "Who is working on DSPM" is worth
answering because the answer already exists, spread across a dozen dictations
nobody has time to re-read. "Draft this to Sanjay" is worth answering because
Kivi has read every message this person sent Sanjay.

The second is where memory changes what Kivi does, not just what it holds.
Kivi learns each relationship on its own — two people in the same
role get written to differently — and keeps learning as that changes. Style
decides wording only; it never supplies a fact the person did not give. And
Kivi never learns from its own drafts.

### What deserves to be remembered

Only what the person says about their own world: their preferences, their
commitments, and the people and projects that come up again and again. Never a
single mention — a misheard name is said once, a real colleague keeps coming
back.

A preference said outright is believed straight away. One merely noticed in how
they write is a guess, and stays marked as a guess for good. A commitment counts
from one mention if the person made it themselves and gave it a date. A direct
instruction — "add Ashwin to DSPM" — is believed at once; nobody should have to
say a thing twice to be believed.

Everything else stays a dictation. Still searchable, never promoted to a belief.

### What Kivi must never assume

Kivi must never treat something said about another person as a fact about them.
Most dictation is about someone else — a colleague, a patient, a customer. The
speaker is the source, not the subject, and none of it becomes memory however
often it comes up.

It must never build a picture of the person themselves. Kivi records what
someone does in the world, not what kind of person they are. Reading character
out of how they talk to their assistant is the easiest impressive thing to build
here, and it is what turns memory into surveillance.

And it must never keep what could be used against someone — health,
relationships, money, employment, character — even when truthfully said.

### Where memory belongs

In Hey Kivi, almost entirely. Dictation must stay predictable: it writes down
what was said, and memory may not quietly improve it. The one thing dictation
contributes is noticing who a message was addressed to — which is what style is
learned from. Recall, drafting and correction happen only when the person asks.

### Why someone should trust this system enough to keep using it

Because every answer can be checked. A fact never appears without the dictation
or instruction behind it, one click away. Where there is no evidence, Kivi says
so instead of inventing. Where the record disagrees with itself, it asks rather
than picking a side.

And because the rules are code, not requests in a prompt. What Kivi refuses to
keep, it refuses every time. Correcting it is a sentence — "forget that" — not
a list of facts to manage. Trust here is not a claim about the model. It is a
claim about what the system will not do.
